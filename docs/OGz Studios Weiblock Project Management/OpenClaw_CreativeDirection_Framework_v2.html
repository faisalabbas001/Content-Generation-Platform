The Three-Axis Creative Direction Framework

A grounded classification system for assigning creative direction to any Saudi SME, built on three independent axes — brand archetype, lifecycle stage, and intent state — yielding 300 possible configurations and a composition algorithm that determines which method (or hybrid composition) fits each one.

Why three axes, not one

The question "which method fits which brand" has been asked at the wrong altitude. Every brand is the intersection of three independent variables, and a complete framework must classify all three before any toolkit assignment becomes meaningful.

This document grounds the system in established frameworks — Jungian brand archetypes (40+ years of literature), product lifecycle theory (60+ years), and customer-intent classification (15+ years of operational research) — composed into a single decision architecture that fits the OpenClaw platform.

1
The Personality Axis
Brand Archetype
What the brand IS — its core character, set at founding, rarely changes. 12 Jungian archetypes, of which 4 dominate Saudi SME frequency.
2
The Stability Axis
Lifecycle Stage
Where the brand IS — pre-launch through recovery. Determines voice modulation, content frequency, and vulnerability permission. 5 stages.
3
The Direction Axis
Intent State
What the brand is TRYING TO DO right now — fluid, can change month to month. Governs chain-mix shift and immediate creative emphasis. 5 states.

12 archetypes × 5 lifecycle stages × 5 intent states = 300 distinct brand configurations. The five existing methods cover 180 of these well at chain-execution depth. With the proposed Vulnerability Method, coverage rises to 240. The remaining 60 require composition logic — pulling patterns from multiple methods rather than assigning one.

Axis 3 — Intent State

The most fluid axis. A brand can shift intent state month to month while archetype and stage remain stable. Each intent governs a distinct chain-mix and creative emphasis.

Launch
"Establish that you exist and matter"
Chain mix: 30% brand story, 35% product, 15% discovery, 10% conversion, 10% engagement
Grow
"Expand reach beyond current audience"
Chain mix: 40% product crave, 25% brand/lifestyle, 15% promotional, 15% occasion, 5% new
Defend
"Protect position from competitive pressure"
Chain mix: 30% differentiator, 25% loyalty/proof, 20% product hero, 15% occasion, 10% conversion
Harvest
"Convert engaged audience into revenue"
Chain mix: 40% product, 25% conversion, 20% promotional, 10% occasion, 5% loyalty
Recover
"Re-engage after dormancy or crisis"
Chain mix: 35% renewed identity, 25% testimonial/proof, 20% behind-the-scenes, 10% comeback launch, 10% occasion

What a method actually contains

This is the architectural shift that resolves the question. A method is not a person. A method is a composed instruction set with five independently classifiable components. Each component is separately addressable, separately swappable, and can be sourced from different methods simultaneously.

The five components

Method anatomy

01
Diagnostic
The question this method asks of every brief. Determines what the system looks for in BrandDNA before producing anything. The Diagnostic method's "what cultural contract is the brand breaking?" runs upstream of all others.
02
Patterns
The repeatable structures the method can execute. Authenticity's two-scene contrast, Paradox's build-and-flip, Heritage's spoken-poetry rhythm. Addressable, separable, composable.
03
Register
The language register the method operates in. Classical MSA + Saudi colloquial (Heritage). Saudi colloquial dominant with English slips (Vulnerability). Tone of voice, independent of structure.
04
Anti-Patterns
What the method refuses to do. As defining as the positive patterns. Vulnerability refuses exclamation marks, "discover," and unearned taglines. Heritage refuses superlatives.
05
Application Rules
Which chains apply, at what intent state, at what brand stage. Authenticity's manifesto applies to brand-level posts. Paradox's build-and-flip applies to milestone posts. Granular routing.

How the system runs at onboarding

Four passes execute in roughly 90 seconds during N8N-A03. The output is a written creative direction object in BrandDNA Family A that governs every chain execution downstream — and updates with every revision cycle as the system calibrates to the brand.

01
Archetype Detection
COO agent reads extracted BrandDNA + onboarding answers. Runs 12-archetype matching. Each archetype scored 0–100. Primary = highest. Secondary if within 10 points.
02
Stage Detection
Five stages scored on observable signals — follower count, posting history depth, branch count, brand registration date, asset library depth. Output: one stage with confidence %.
03
Intent Detection
Onboarding Q2 + Q5 + extracted differentiator field. Output: one of five intent states with confidence percentage. Re-runs monthly as the brand evolves.
04
Method Composition
Match (archetype × stage × intent) tuple against the matrix. If single method scores 80+: assign as primary. If not: pull strongest patterns from top three scoring methods. Synthesize hybrid.

What this asks Phase 1 to do

Three concrete decisions before the BrandDNA schema is locked.

One. Add three fields to BrandDNA Family A — archetype_primary, archetype_secondary, and lifecycle_stage — to complete the (archetype × stage × intent) tuple. Intent state already exists in the framework.

Two. Encode methods as composable pattern objects rather than monolithic voice settings. Each method becomes the five-component anatomy described above, with each pattern separately addressable for hybrid composition.

Three. Build the Vulnerability Method as the system-default for Launch-stage SMEs and the composition fallback for any (archetype × stage × intent) configuration where no single method scores above 80. This single addition is the highest-leverage architectural decision available in Phase 1.
