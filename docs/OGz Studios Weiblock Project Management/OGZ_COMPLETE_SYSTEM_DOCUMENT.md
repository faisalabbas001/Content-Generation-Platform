# PART 1 — THE PLATFORM

## 1.1 What OGz AI Is

OGz AI is Saudi Arabia's first content operating system. It does not generate generic AI content. It builds a living intelligence profile for each brand — its voice, its visual grammar, its cultural positioning, its audience, its competitive context — and uses that intelligence to generate culturally precise Arabic content automatically, for every occasion, across every platform, getting more accurate every month it runs.

Saudi Arabia has 34.1 million social media users — equal to 99.6% of the total population. TikTok's ad reach covers 138% of the adult population, placing the Kingdom among the top countries worldwide. Snapchat reached 25.3 million users in Saudi Arabia at the end of 2025, with 89% of adults aged 18 and above using the platform. Instagram is the second most popular platform with users forecasted to reach 23.5 million by 2025.

Every Saudi brand operating in this landscape faces the same problem: producing consistent, culturally correct, Arabic-first content at the pace social platforms demand is expensive, slow, and dependent on individual creative talent. The market produces generic. OGz AI produces specific.

**The product is not content. The product is BrandDNA — the accumulated intelligence that makes content specific.**

## 1.2 The Two Pipelines

### Pipeline A — Starter

Self-serve. Brand onboards through a guided conversation. The system extracts their social media history, analyses their assets, asks what it cannot find, then builds a full content strategy and 3-month calendar automatically. Brand approves post by post. Platform generates, the brand publishes.

### Pipeline B — Growth / Enterprise

Managed. Same system, deeper intelligence. Full BrandDNA built through a richer onboarding session. The platform produces full campaign strategy — multiple pieces, multiple platforms, strategic thinking behind every content decision. Human strategic review before delivery.

## 1.3 The Saudi Platform Landscape

The platform generates content for all major Saudi social platforms. Snapchat holds particular strength in the Saudi market, with the Kingdom ranking among the platform's top markets globally. Instagram maintains a large and commercially active user base. TikTok has grown rapidly, particularly among younger demographics. LinkedIn has grown as professional networking and corporate branding gain importance in the Kingdom's evolving business culture.

Short-form video content has become increasingly popular with the rise of TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. Ad spending on short-form videos is expected to reach a market volume of $127.2 million by 2028.

The platform's content strategy defaults are built around this landscape. Every brand gets a platform-appropriate content mix by default, with the ability to shift weight toward their strongest platform.

# PART 2 — BRANDDNA

## 2.1 What BrandDNA Is

BrandDNA is the complete living intelligence file for a brand. Every agent, every generation, every strategy decision reads from it. Nothing about the brand lives outside it. It has six layers.

## 2.2 The Six Layers

### Layer 1 — Brand Identity

The structural facts about the brand. Updated rarely.

| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sector                | F&B / Retail / Beauty / Real Estate / Healthcare / other         |
| Sub-sector            | Casual dining / Fine dining / Heritage food / Fast casual / etc. |
| Region primary        | Najdi / Hejazi / Eastern / Southern / National                   |
| City                  | Primary city of operation                                        |
| Founded               | Year founded                                                     |
| Language preference   | Arabic first / Bilingual / Platform-specific                     |
| Dialect               | Najdi-leaning / Hejazi-leaning / MSA / Mixed                     |
| Religious sensitivity | Standard / Conservative / None                                   |
| Tone register         | Traditional / Modern / Youth / Mixed                             |
| Anti-attributes       | What the brand is NOT — never appears in content                |
| Style register        | The visual register the brand operates in                        |

### Layer 2 — Owner Profile

The person behind the brand. Especially critical for SMEs where the brand is inseparable from the founder.

| Field                     | Description                                                |

# PART 3 — THE ONBOARDING FLOW

## 3.1 Onboarding Philosophy

The system does most of the work. The client answers as few questions as possible. The system extracts from their links, learns from their assets, and only asks what it cannot find.

For a new brand with no social media history, the system guides them. It shows them what works for their sector from ogz-knowledge and helps them understand their strategic options before asking them to make decisions.

## 3.2 The Exact Sequence

```
STEP 1: ACCOUNT CREATION AND LINKS
Client creates account. Provides:
  - Business name (Arabic + English)
  - Sector and city
  - All social media handles (Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, YouTube, X)
  - Competitor handles (3-5)
  - Website if exists

STEP 2: AUTOMATIC EXTRACTION (runs in background, 3-5 minutes)
While the client sees a progress screen and guided questions:
  - Full social media extraction from all provided handles
  - Competitor account extraction (light — posting patterns, engagement, content categories)
  - Website extraction if provided
  - ogz-knowledge sector baseline loaded

STEP 3: GUIDED QUESTIONS (only what extraction couldn't find)
The system asks ONLY what it needs and couldn't extract:
  - What makes you different from competitors? (if not clear from content)
  - What are you NOT? (anti-attributes — always asked)
  - What is your dialect and city focus? (if ambiguous)
  - Religious sensitivity level? (always asked — cannot infer safely)
  - Who is your primary customer? (age, gender, lifestyle)
  - What are you trying to achieve in the next 3 months?

STEP 4: BUSINESS EVENTS AND KEY DATES
Before strategy is built:
  - Any product launches in next 3 months?
  - Any promotions or seasonal campaigns planned?
  - Any store openings or events?
  - Any dates or periods to avoid?
  These events anchor the content calendar.

STEP 5: OWNER PERSONA (first questions)
The system begins learning about the owner:
  - Would you like to appear in content?
  - Do you have team members who can appear in content?
  - What is your brand's founding story? (brief)
  This continues in the ongoing chat — not all captured at onboarding.

STEP 6: ASSETS UPLOAD
  - Logo files
  - Product photos (however many they have)
  - Location/space photos
  - Team photos
  - Any existing brand materials
  System processes all assets and adds to BrandDNA Layer 3.

STEP 7: STRATEGY DRAFT BUILT
System builds the first strategy draft using:
  - All extracted data
  - All uploaded assets
  - Onboarding answers
  - Sector defaults from ogz-knowledge
  - Saudi occasion calendar
  - Business events provided
  Takes 3-5 minutes. Client sees progress indicator.

STEP 8: STRATEGY DRAFT REVIEW
Client sees the draft strategy. They can comment on any section.
System defends its decisions with data when client disagrees.
Client has the final call but the system argues its position clearly.

STEP 9: STRATEGY FINAL
Once approved: strategy is locked as v1.0.
Calendar generation begins immediately.
```

## 3.3 New Brands With No History

When a brand has no social media presence, the system:

- Loads the full sector baseline from ogz-knowledge as the starting point
- Shows the client anonymised examples of what works for brands like them
- Asks more questions than it would for an established brand
- Builds a starter strategy based on sector defaults until real performance data accumulates
- The first 90 days are the calibration period — the system is explicit about this with the client

# PART 4 — THE STRATEGY SYSTEM

## 4.1 What the Strategy Layer Does

In 2026, content strategy is no longer a human-planned calendar — it is an intelligent system that learns, evolves, and executes. AI connects creativity, analytics, and automation into a seamless loop where content adapts continuously.

The strategy layer sits between the brief and the chain selection. Without it, the system produces technically correct content with no strategic direction. With it, every piece of content serves a purpose.

The strategy answers three questions before any content is generated:

1. What is this brand trying to say — not the surface ask, but the real human truth?
2. What cultural tension does this brand have permission to own?
3. Is this the right moment for safe content or brave content?

## 4.2 The 8-Step Strategic Reasoning Model

The system runs this model for every new campaign brief and every strategy refresh:

```
Step 1: Reject the surface brief
        Find the real human ask beneath the client ask.
        "Promote our new coffee blend" →
        "Give our regulars a reason to bring someone new"

Step 2: Name the cultural tension
        What is the brand navigating?
        Pride vs. invisibility
        Tradition vs. modernity
        Personal vs. commercial
        Aspiration vs. belonging

Step 3: Define the permission level
        Category leader: can shape category conversation
        Challenger: can disrupt conventions
        Institutional: must respect authority and trust
        Purpose: leads with values
        Launch: earns permission through newness
        SME local: authenticity and community are the permission

Step 4: Select the creative formula
        Manifesto build — stakes a claim for a truth
        Paradox play — holds two true things in tension
        Freeze then action — stops time, then moves
        Scene pair — two moments that mean something together
        Number reframe — makes data emotionally true
        Gamified hook — makes participation irresistible
        Metaphor system — sees the category differently

Step 5: Pick register and dialect deliberately
        MSA formal — institutional, government-adjacent
        MSA accessible — national brand, broad audience
        Najdi colloquial — Riyadh-native, youth-adjacent
        Hejazi colloquial — Jeddah-native, coastal warmth
        Mixed bilingual — modern Saudi professional
        Code-switching — intentional Arabic-English mixing

Step 6: Test against anti-attributes and policy rules
        Does this contradict what the brand is NOT?
        Does this touch any hard block territory?
        Does this require a brave route release?

Step 7: Generate safe route AND brave route
        Safe: executes the strategy within comfortable territory
        Brave: pushes the cultural tension — needs human review if risk markers fire

Step 8: Score confidence before release
        Fluency is not confidence.
        A beautiful Arabic line that misses the cultural truth scores low.
```

## 4.3 Permission Levels — What They Mean in Practice

| Level           | Who It's For                             | What's Allowed                               | What's Not                                         |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Category leader | National brand, dominant in sector       | Can define what the category means           | Cannot be seen as arrogant or dismissive of others |
| Challenger      | Growing brand, disrupting the default    | Can call out the generic, the safe, the lazy | Cannot punch at specific competitors by name       |
| Institutional   | Government-adjacent, authority-dependent | Can speak with weight and gravity            | Cannot be experimental or ironic                   |
| Purpose         | Values-driven brand                      | Can take positions on cultural moments       | Cannot be inconsistent with stated values          |
| Launch          | New to market                            | Can be bold through newness                  | Cannot claim authority not yet earned              |
| SME local       | Neighbourhood brand, community-rooted    | Can be intimate, personal, specific          | Cannot pretend to be bigger than they are          |

## 4.4 The Strategy as a Living Document

The strategy is never finished. It updates when:

| Trigger                               | What Updates                                                         | How Client Finds Out                                                             |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Post performs 3x above average        | Pattern winner added to Layer 6, content mix shifts toward that type | Notification: "This type of content is working. We're making more of it."        |
| Post performs 3x below average        | Pattern loser flagged, formula or register reviewed                  | Notification: "This type of content underperformed. Here's what we're changing." |
| Competitor makes a significant move   | Competitive intelligence updated, gap analysis refreshed             | Alert: "Your competitor did X. Here's how you can own a different space."        |
| Saudi occasion approaching (30 days)  | Calendar adjusted for occasion phase, register shifted               | Calendar update with occasion content inserted automatically                     |
| Business event added                  | Calendar rebuilds around the new event                               | Client sees updated calendar for approval                                        |
| Performance data accumulates (weekly) | Benchmarks updated, content mix refined                              | Weekly summary in the performance dashboard                                      |
| Strategy version number increments    | Client receives notification with summary of what changed and why    | "Your strategy updated: here's what changed and why."                            |

## 4.5 Defending the Strategy

When a client disagrees with a strategic recommendation, the system responds as an expert — not as an assistant. It does not simply agree because the client pushed back.

The defence uses all available data:

- Sector benchmarks from ogz-knowledge: "Brands in your sector that used this approach saw X% higher engagement"
- The brand's own history: "Your content from the past 3 months shows that your audience responds better to X than Y"
- Competitive intelligence: "Your main competitor tried this angle and here's what happened"
- Cultural rationale: "This register works for your city and audience because..."

The system states its case clearly, once, with evidence. Then the client decides. The system logs whether the client accepted or overrode the recommendation — this becomes part of the learning record.

# PART 5 — THE CONTENT CALENDAR

## 5.1 Calendar Architecture

The calendar is 3 months, rolling. When month 1 completes, month 4 is automatically generated and added. The calendar always shows 3 months ahead.

Each calendar slot contains:

- Content type (product / lifestyle / occasion / brand story / founder / competitor response)
- Platform (Instagram / TikTok / Snapchat / YouTube / all)
- Format (image / video / story / reel)
- Chain assigned (which of the 88 chains will execute this)
- Creative formula (which strategic approach)
- Status (scheduled / generating / ready for approval / approved / published / missed)
- Strategic note (one line: why this post is in this slot)

## 5.2 How the Calendar Is Built

```
INPUTS:
  - Brand's goal phase (awareness / conversion / retention / launch)
  - Content mix ratios from BrandDNA Layer 4
  - Saudi occasion calendar — occasions auto-inserted
  - Business events from client — anchor points
  - Platform weights from brand profile
  - Performance history — what types work for this brand

OCCASION LOGIC:
  Ramadan: 3 phases — pre-arrival (14 days), active (30 days), post-Eid transition
           Entire content register shifts during Ramadan
           Product content reduces, occasion content increases
           Client sets their Ramadan weight during strategy approval

  National Day (23 Sept): 2-week build, day-of peak, 3-day tail
  Founding Day (22 Feb): same structure
  Eid Al-Fitr / Eid Al-Adha: 3-day peak, 1-week tail each

  All occasions auto-inserted.
  Client can reduce occasion weight in strategy settings.
  Client cannot remove occasions entirely without explicit decision.

GOAL PHASE LOGIC:
  Awareness phase: more brand story, more founder, more cultural content
  Conversion phase: more product, more offer, more CTA-driven
  Retention phase: more community, more behind-the-scenes, more loyal customer content
  Launch phase: announcement-led, excitement-building, exclusive-feel

CONTENT MIX DEFAULT BY SECTOR (from ogz-knowledge):
  F&B: 30% product, 25% lifestyle, 20% occasion, 15% behind-the-scenes, 10% founder
  Retail: 35% product, 20% lifestyle, 20% occasion, 15% campaign, 10% brand story
  Beauty: 30% product demo, 25% lifestyle, 20% occasion, 15% transformation, 10% founder
  Real Estate: 25% property, 30% lifestyle, 20% brand story, 15% occasion, 10% milestone
  Healthcare: 30% education, 25% trust-building, 20% occasion, 15% behind-the-scenes, 10% milestone
```

## 5.3 The Two Content Types

Every calendar slot is one of two types:

**Type 1 — System Generated**
The platform builds this content end-to-end. It selects the chain, composes the prompt, generates through fal.ai, runs compliance, captions in the right dialect and register, and delivers ready for client approval.

**Type 2 — Client Created**
The client films or photographs their own content and uploads it. The platform:

- Accepts the upload
- Runs compliance check (hard blocks first, then soft flags)
- Generates caption in the right dialect and register
- Suggests improvements if compliance issues found
- Can edit video backgrounds or fix visual compliance issues (with client approval before touching)
- Slots it into the calendar at the strategically optimal time
- Tracks its performance as part of the learning loop

**How Type 2 slots appear in the calendar:**

- Client manually marks a slot as "I'll create this"
- System suggests which slots benefit most from client-created content: "This week, a founder video in this slot would outperform a generated alternative — would you like to reserve it?"
- Client can also upload outside the calendar through the creation tab

## 5.4 Content Created One Week Before Posting

All system-generated content is produced 7 days before its scheduled posting date. This creates:

- Time for OGZ internal review (compliance, quality, strategy fit)
- Time for client approval
- Buffer for revision if needed

**The approval flow:**

```
Day -7: System generates content for next week
Day -7: OGZ internal reviewer sees content in internal dashboard
         Reviews for: compliance, quality, strategy fit, brand consistency
         Approves / flags for regeneration / notes for client attention
Day -6 to -2: Client receives notification
              Client reviews post by post
              Approves / rejects / requests change
Day -1: Final deadline for client approval
Day 0:  Approved content scheduled for posting at optimal time
        Unapproved content: HELD — client notified with urgency
```

**Auto-post option:**
Client can enable auto-post by signing a responsibility agreement. Once enabled:

- They can set rules: auto-post product content / require approval for occasion content
- Auto-post only activates for content that passed OGZ review and the confidence threshold
- Client can revoke auto-post at any time
- All auto-posted content is logged with the auto-post flag

## 5.5 The Creation Tab

Available at any time. Separate from the calendar but connected to it.

The client opens the creation tab when they want to create something outside the calendar schedule. Two modes:

**Guided mode (recommended):**
The system walks the client through a creation session step by step:

1. "What are you trying to achieve with this post?"
2. "Which platform?"
3. "Do you want to use your own photo/video, or should I generate it?"
4. "Here are 3 strategic angles that fit your brand and this moment — which resonates?"
5. System generates based on chosen direction
6. Client reviews and adjusts
7. System offers to add to calendar at the best available slot

**Freeform mode:**
Client types a brief directly. System generates. Adjusts to brand strategy automatically — nothing produced in the creation tab exists outside the brand strategy framework. The post still follows the brand's dialect, register, anti-attributes, and cultural constraints. Nothing is "off-brand" even in freeform mode.

All content from the creation tab is offered a calendar slot. Nothing is wasted.

# PART 6 — THE FOUNDER CONTENT PIPELINE

## 6.1 Why Founder Content Matters

The most engaging content for Saudi SMEs is content featuring the real person behind the brand. An authentic founder video or a real behind-the-scenes moment consistently outperforms AI-generated equivalents for brands where trust and personality are the differentiator.

Human-generated content still receives 5.44x more traffic than purely AI-generated content. AI campaigns deliver 32% more conversions and 22% higher ROI — but the winning model is human-AI collaboration, not AI alone.

## 6.2 How the System Handles Founder Content

During onboarding and through the ongoing chat, the system learns:

- Whether the owner wants to appear in content
- Whether they want to film the space or team instead
- Their comfort level in front of camera
- What they find authentic vs forced

When the content calendar has a slot suited to founder content, the system:

1. Proposes the idea to the client: "This week would be a strong moment for a founder video — here's the angle that fits the strategy"
2. If accepted: generates a full shot brief — not just a script, a production guide
3. Delivers the brief to the client

**The shot brief includes:**

- The strategic angle (why this content, why now)
- The hook (the first 3 seconds)
- What to say (full script in the client's language and dialect)
- Where to film (specific setting guidance)
- What to wear (general guidance, consistent with brand register)
- Lighting (time of day, window vs artificial, what to avoid)
- Camera (phone position, distance, orientation)
- Duration (optimal length for the platform)
- The CTA (what to say at the end)

## 6.3 When LoRA Is Used

In early stages: real person filming always preferred. The shot brief system handles this.

In later stages (when the brand has a strong visual identity established):

- The brand can request a LoRA trained on the owner's appearance
- System guides the owner through providing training photos (20+ photos, multiple angles, multiple lighting conditions, multiple expressions)
- LoRA is trained and stored in BrandDNA Layer 3
- Used for AI-generated content featuring the owner when real filming is not possible
- Hard limit: the LoRA is never used to generate the owner in any situation they have not explicitly approved as a content type

# PART 7 — COMPETITOR INTELLIGENCE

## 7.1 What the System Tracks

At onboarding, the client provides 3-5 competitor handles. The system begins tracking immediately.

**Initial extraction (light):**

- Posting frequency and rhythm
- Platform presence
- Content category distribution
- Estimated engagement rate
- Occasion approach

**Ongoing tracking (deepening over time):**

- New content patterns detected
- Engagement shifts
- New platforms joined
- Campaign launches
- Occasion strategy changes

**Full cultural extraction (for top competitors):**
Same extraction spec as ogz-knowledge observations. Run on the top 2 competitors quarterly. Provides deep insight into what their content is actually doing culturally, not just what it is.

## 7.2 The Two Types of Competitor Alerts

**Immediate alert:**
Competitor launches something that requires a response — a major campaign, a price change announced publicly, a product launch, a cultural moment they are owning that this brand should respond to. Sent immediately to the client with suggested content direction.

**Weekly summary:**
Part of the performance dashboard. Shows competitor performance trends, what's working for them, what gaps they're leaving open. No immediate action required — strategic context only.

## 7.3 Competitive Content Suggestions

When the system detects a competitive opportunity:

- "Your competitor is getting strong results on heritage content. Here is how you can own a different angle on the same territory."
- "Your competitor is absent from Snapchat. Your audience is there. This is an opportunity."
- "Your competitor's engagement dropped after switching to this format. Consider this instead."

These suggestions appear as proactive calendar recommendations. Client accepts, adjusts, or ignores. All decisions are logged.

# PART 8 — PERFORMANCE TRACKING

## 8.1 What Is Tracked

Client connects their social media accounts during onboarding. The system accesses analytics data in real time. All data flows into BrandDNA Layer 6.

**Per post:**

- Reach and impressions
- Engagement rate (likes + comments + saves + shares / reach)
- Saves (strongest signal for quality content)
- Shares (strongest signal for cultural resonance)
- Comments (sentiment and content logged)
- Profile visits from this post
- Link clicks if applicable
- Video completion rate (for video content)

**Per platform:**

- Follower growth rate
- Audience demographics (age, gender, location)
- Best performing posting times
- Content category performance

**Benchmarks (three levels):**

1. This brand's own historical average — how is this post doing vs their own baseline?
2. Sector benchmark from ogz-knowledge — how is this brand doing vs similar Saudi brands?
3. Platform benchmark — how is this performing vs platform averages for Saudi accounts?

## 8.2 How Performance Feeds Strategy

Performance data is the primary driver of strategy updates. The system does not wait for a scheduled review.

| Signal                                                     | System Action                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Post hits 3x average engagement                            | Pattern added to winners, content mix shifts toward this type, client notified |
| Post hits 0.3x average engagement                          | Pattern flagged, formula or register reviewed, client notified                 |
| Video completion rate drops across 3+ videos               | Pacing or hook format reviewed, prompt template adjusted                       |
| Saves consistently high on one content type                | Type promoted in content mix, more slots allocated                             |
| Competitor outperforming on a gap this brand isn't filling | Competitive alert triggered, calendar slot suggested                           |
| Occasion content underperforms vs sector benchmark         | Occasion approach reviewed in strategy, client asked about adjusting weight    |

The AI Marketing Strategy automates brainstorming, scheduling, and reporting while freeing up human resources for high-level planning and strategy. A centralized platform with instant feedback loops decreases bottlenecks and increases response rates.

# PART 9 — THE AGENT ARCHITECTURE

## 9.1 Design Principle

No agents make creative or filmmaking decisions. The chains encode the creative approach. The cultural specs from ogz-knowledge encode the visual grammar. Agents handle intake, execution, compliance, learning. Not creativity.

## 9.2 The Spine — 6 Deterministic Components

These run deterministically. No LLM. They enforce rules that no agent can override.

| Component         | Job                                                                                       |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Memory Controller | The only write path to BrandDNA and ogz-knowledge. All agent writes go through this.      |
| Compliance Gate   | Hard block enforcement on every generated output. Cannot be bypassed.                     |
| Budget Enforcer   | Tracks per-brand generation spend. Blocks when limit reached.                             |
| Event Logger      | Append-only log of every generation, approval, rejection, compliance event.               |
| Calendar Engine   | Saudi occasion awareness — what occasion is active, what phase, how many days remaining. |
| Schema Validator  | Every record must pass validation before entering the database.                           |

## 9.3 Client-Facing Agents (8 agents)

These serve clients. The client experiences one coherent platform — not 8 agents.

| Agent                    | Model      | Job                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------ | ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Platform Assistant (CEO) | Sonnet 4.6 | The single voice the client interacts with. Takes brief, routes production, delivers output, defends strategy. |
| Brand Onboarding Guide   | Sonnet 4.6 | Conducts the intake session. Extracts from links, asks only what's missing, builds the initial BrandDNA.       |
| Strategy Agent           | Sonnet 4.6 | Runs the 8-step reasoning model. Builds strategy draft. Defends recommendations. Updates living strategy.      |
| Prompt Composer          | Sonnet 4.6 | Most critical agent. Synthesizes BrandDNA + cultural spec + chain + brief into the fal.ai prompt.              |
| COO (Executor)           | Haiku 4.5  | Executes fal.ai calls. Manages retries. Returns generated asset.                                               |
| CCO (Cultural Review)    | Sonnet 4.6 | Reviews every output for Arabic quality and Saudi cultural compliance.                                         |
| Arabic Validator         | Sonnet 4.6 | When Arabic text is present: validates spelling, dialect, RTL, register.                                       |
| Confidence Scorer        | Haiku 4.5  | Takes CCO result + Arabic validation + brand history → final score and delivery routing.                      |

## 9.4 System Agents (5 agents — invisible to clients)

## 9.5 Chain Selection — No Agent, Pure Logic

Chain selection is deterministic. No LLM reasoning involved.

```
1. FILTER: Keep chains where sector_affinity matches + platform matches + format matches
   Result: 8-15 eligible chains from 88

2. BOOST: Active occasion → chains tagged for that occasion get +20 score

3. SCORE: Remaining chains scored by:
   - Style register match (0-30 points)
   - Brand's historical approval rate for this chain (0-40 points)
   - Platform-wide approval rate for this chain (0-30 points)

4. SELECT: Top 3 chains returned to Prompt Composer

5. VERIFY: Chain approval rate monitored continuously
   Below 60% → chain flagged for review
   Below 40% → chain suspended, Learning Agent investigates
```

# PART 10 — ogz-knowledge

## 10.1 What It Is

`ogz-knowledge` is the file-first knowledge repository. It is a Git repository of structured YAML and JSON files. The Supabase database indexes these files — it does not replace them. Git is the source of truth.

**Current state:** v1.0.0 — 400 files, 1.24MB, GitHub `rabie011/ogz-knowledge` (private)

## 10.2 Architecture Principles

| Principle             | What It Means                                                                     |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| File-first            | Every record is a file in Git. Database indexes files, never replaces them.       |
| One write path        | Only the Memory Controller writes. No agent writes directly.                      |
| Append-only           | Records are never edited after creation. New versions are new files.              |
| Quarantine not delete | Bad records move to `_quarantine/`. Nothing deleted.                            |
| ULIDs everywhere      | Every record has a ULID as its primary identifier.                                |
| 5 provenance fields   | Every record: source, date_added, confirmer, confidence, scope.                   |
| Brand isolation       | Client BrandDNA is RLS-isolated in Supabase. Cross-brand contamination prevented. |
| Confidence levels     | experimental → inferred → confirmed. Only Cultural Advisor confirms.            |

# PART 11 — COMPLIANCE AND CONTENT GOVERNANCE

## 11.1 The Hard Block List

Ten violations that are never published. Detected automatically after every generation.

| Code                                  | Violation                                                            |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `left_hand_serving`                 | Left hand serving food or beverages                                  |
| `left_hand_exchange`                | Left hand for formal object exchange                                 |
| `sole_pointing`                     | Sole of foot pointed at a person                                     |
| `beckoning_palm_up`                 | Palm-up beckoning gesture                                            |
| `cross_gender_contact`              | Physical contact between non-mahrams in traditional-register content |
| `food_consumption_ramadan_daylight` | Food consumed during Ramadan daylight hours                          |
| `quran_mishandling`                 | Quran placed under other objects or mishandled                       |
| `index_finger_pointing`             | Index finger pointing directly at a person                           |
| `western_head_shake_no`             | Western head-shake for "no"                                          |
| `counting_wrong_sequence`           | Counting starting at index finger                                    |

## 11.2 The CCO Cultural Review

Every output after the hard block gate passes through the CCO agent:

- Arabic text quality (spelling, dialect, RTL, register, diacritics)
- Visual compliance (wardrobe micro-details, proxemics, gestures)
- Cultural fit with the brand's sector and register
- Soft flags (not violations — things that could be more Saudi)
- Confidence score

## 11.3 Client-Uploaded Content

When a client uploads their own video or image:

1. Hard block gate runs first
2. If hard block: content rejected, client notified with specific reason
3. If soft flag: system recommends specific fix — "we noticed X, here is how we can improve it"
4. If the system can fix it (background change, crop, colour adjustment): system proposes the specific edit with a preview — client approves before the system touches the file
5. Client has the final call on whether to fix or post as-is
6. If posted as-is with known soft flags: flagged in performance tracking for learning

## 11.4 Religious Content

Religious content always routes to human review. No exceptions. Never auto-published regardless of confidence score.

## 11.5 The OGZ Internal Review

Every piece of content, 7 days before posting, is reviewed by an OGZ-appointed reviewer through the internal dashboard. The reviewer sees:

- The generated content
- The confidence score
- All compliance flags
- The strategic reasoning behind this post
- The brand's BrandDNA summary
- The chain and formula used

The reviewer can: approve / flag for regeneration / add a note for the client / escalate to Mohamed.
